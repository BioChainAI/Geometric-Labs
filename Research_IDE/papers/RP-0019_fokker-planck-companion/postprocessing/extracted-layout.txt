         Abstract
         This companion holds everything about the Fokker-Planck prime sieve that is not
         its statement and proof. The sieve itself, its proofs and the standard it supports
         are in the release paper (Hoard 2026e). Here are the relation of that paper to its
         three earlier versions and the sixteen questions those versions raised; the
         consolidated status of 72 claims of the source note, of the nine companion papers
         of the SHD-CCP programme and of the release itself; the audit of the source note;
         and the corrections of the companion papers, with complete proofs where a
         correction rests on a theorem. Of the companion papers' claims, the golden
         solitons, the one-sided delta, the 720-step guarantee for arbitrary deltas, the three
         earlier packet layouts and the South Halo as an integrity check do not survive. The
         trefoil's D₃ symmetry, periodic closure in the binary polyhedral groups and the
         coherence score survive in corrected form. The 720-step cycle is examined with
         the telescoping identity of the cubit installment, which shows that the cyclic
         holonomy is identically 1 and so cannot test closure. Ten open problems are stated
         in the manner of a registered report: each with its status, the evidence so far, a
         test and a decision rule ﬁxed before any new computation or measurement, and a
         note on whether the release depends on it. It depends on none of them.

 Epistemic Conventions and Scope
 The companion uses the ten tags of Hoard (2026e), Table 1, and its rule for mixed claims: a
 computed value of a heuristic formula is tagged [HEURISTIC / COMPUTED]. A claim of a
 source keeps the tag that the source's own argument supports, which is not always the tag the
 source gave itself.

 What this companion contains. The history of the installment (Section 1), the status of
 every claim (Section 2), the audit of the source note (Section 3), the corrections of the
 companion papers (Section 4) and the open problems (Section 5). It proves results only where
 a correction rests on them.

 What it does not do. It does not restate the sieve; it cites Hoard (2026e) for every positive
 result. It reviews the companion papers only where the sieve uses them, so a claim not listed
 here is neither endorsed nor refuted.

 Veriﬁcation. Every number here is reproduced by the script of Hoard (2026e), Appendix A;
 Section 6 lists the sections that check this companion.

 1.3 Corrections Between Versions
        Prime coverage of the Form 1 core. A draft of the revision stated that the Form 1 core
        reaches all 1,877 primes up to 16,129. The count had included primes with no encodable
        core. The correct ﬁgure is 1,712 of 1,877; the other 165 need a coordinate outside the
        byte range (Hoard 2026e, Section 8.2; Appendix A, section Y).
        Citation labels. The second edition cited the companion papers with Hoard labels. From
        the revision on they are cited as BioChain AI (2026a–i), as the Fifteen Lemmas cite them,
        so that Hoard (2026e) can denote this installment.
        Packet reading. The second edition read a 32-bit byte core of four signed bytes; the
        revision replaced it by the Form 1 core of the Einstein Tile. Section R of the veriﬁcation
        script keeps the earlier statistics for comparison.
        Numbering. In the release, the second edition's Theorem 6.3 and Proposition 6.4 are
        Theorem 6.1 and Proposition 6.2; its Proposition 6.1 and Theorem 6.2 are Proposition 4.1
        and Theorem 4.2 here; its Sections 8.4 and 11 are Sections 4.7 and 2 here, and its
        Appendix A is divided between Sections 3 and 4 here; and its Appendices B and C are
        Appendices A and B of Hoard (2026e).

 4.2 Quaternion Matrices and Inverses
 Correction (Quaternion Matrices). BioChain AI (2026d), §2.2, calls the 4 × 4 matrix of q =
 w + xi + yj + zk a real skew-symmetric matrix. It is the matrix L(q) of left multiplication by q,
 which satisﬁes L(q)ᵀL(q) = N(q)I and L(q) + L(q)ᵀ = 2wI: a scaled orthogonal matrix with det
 L(q) = N(q)², skew-symmetric only when w = 0 (Fifteen Lemmas, Lemma 8, Step 2)
 [SUPERSEDED].

 Correction (Inverses). BioChain AI (2026d), §2.1, states that every generated Hurwitz path
 has an inverse. The inverse q̄ /N(q) of a non-unit is not a Hurwitz integer, so the claim holds
 over ℚ only. Exact inversion of a norm-5 word is instead the reversed word of conjugates,
 divided by the scalar 5^L (Hoard 2026e, Deﬁnition 7.1).


 4.3 Trefoil Folding
 The symmetry-folding paper routes three data streams along the trefoil x = sin t + 2 sin 2t, y =
 cos t − 2 cos 2t, z = −sin 3t, and stores one third of it (BioChain AI 2026d, §4).

       Proposition 4.1 (Trefoil Symmetry). [PROVEN] Fifteen Lemmas, Lemma 8 Step
       6; conﬁrms BioChain AI (2026d), §4.2.
       The curve is carried to itself by the rotation of 120° about the z-axis, with t ↦ t −
       2π/3, and by the half-turn about the y-axis, with t ↦ −t. These generate the dihedral
       group D₃ of order 6. With the C₃ axis along (1, 1, 1) and a half-turn axis along (1, −1,
       0), its six elements are integer signed-permutation matrices in the cube group O.

       Proof. Write w(t) = x(t) + iy(t) = ie^{−it} − 2ie^{2it}. Then w(t + 2π/3) =
       e^{−2πi/3}w(t) and z(t + 2π/3) = z(t), a rotation by 120°; and (x, y, z)(−t) = (−x, y,
       −z)(t), the half-turn about y. Conjugating by the orthonormal frame whose second
       axis is (1, −1, 0)/√2 and third axis (1, 1, 1)/√3 turns the two generators into the

       integer matrices with rows (0, 1, 0), (0, 0, 1), (1, 0, 0) and (0, −1, 0), (−1, 0, 0), (0, 0,
       −1). ■

 Hoard (2026e), Appendix A, section N, conﬁrms both identities to 4 × 10⁻¹⁵, and section X that
 the two integer matrices generate six rotations.

 Correction (What Folding Saves). With the full D₃, a fundamental arc is one sixth of the
 curve, not one third: the arc t ∈ [0, π/3] and its six integer images rebuild the whole knot
 (Fifteen Lemmas, Lemma 8 Step 6), so the curve tables shrink by ﬁve sixths. Data written
 along the knot is a diﬀerent matter: folding it is lossless only if the data itself is D₃-symmetric,
 because a map from six segments to one is injective only on symmetric inputs. The source's

 4.4 The 720-Step Cycle
 The source restricts evolution to a 720-step state machine and states that at step 720 "the
 product of all quaternion deltas, trefoil symmetries, and hyperbolic projections mathematically
 guarantees a return to the Identity Matrix" (BioChain AI 2026d, §5). The guarantee holds for a
 deﬁnite class of schedules, and in exact arithmetic that class is small.

       Theorem 4.2 (Finite Delta Groups Close). [PROVEN] Restricts and proves
       BioChain AI (2026d), §5.
       The binary tetrahedral, octahedral and icosahedral groups 2T, 2O and 2I have
       exponents 12, 24 and 60, each dividing 720. Hence if a schedule repeats one ﬁxed
       word w in the deltas of any of these groups, w⁷²⁰ = 1, and already w¹² = 1, w²⁴ = 1 or
       w⁶⁰ = 1 respectively.

       Proof. The element orders are 1, 2, 3, 4 and 6 in 2T; 1, 2, 3, 4, 6 and 8 in 2O; and 1,
       2, 3, 4, 5, 6 and 10 in 2I (Hoard 2026e, Appendix A, section J). Their least common
       multiples are 12, 24 and 60. The word w is a group element, so its power by the
       exponent is 1. ■

 By Hoard (2026e), Theorem 6.1, only the elements of 2T and those of orders 1, 2, 3, 4 and 6 in
 2O and 2I can be realized in exact rational arithmetic, so an exact periodic schedule that
 returns at all returns within 12 repetitions.

 Remark (Arbitrary Deltas Do Not Close). [COMPUTED] When the 720 deltas are drawn
 independently from the 24 Hurwitz units, the product equals 1 in 92 of 2,000 runs (4.6 %),
 close to the 1/24 expected of a uniformly random element. The source's guarantee for arbitrary
 deltas is [SUPERSEDED].

 Remark (The Cyclic Holonomy Is Vacuous). [PROVEN] The cubit installment proves the
 identity behind the guarantee: with the cyclic convention q₋₁ := q₇₁₉ and increments Δq_k =
 q_k q_(k−1)⁻¹, the product of all 720 increments equals 1 for every sequence of states, by
 telescoping (Hoard 2026d, Proposition 4.6(1)). A closure test built from stored states therefore
 cannot fail and tests nothing. The Fifteen Lemmas reach the same verdict from the holonomy
 side: under no boundary convention does the 720-frame cycle force the state back to the
 identity or remove drift, and only the vacuous cyclic convention makes the holonomy
 identically 1 (Lemma 12). A genuine test needs increments generated independently of the
 states, for example by integrating an angular velocity, a stated product order, and a measure


                                                       — 12 —
BioChain AI — SHD-CCP Architecture Research | Consolidated Technical Report (Continued)   Geometric Engine of Time



 such as the rotation angle 2 arccos|Re H| (Hoard 2026d, Remark 4.7); the open convention H =
 q₀q₇₁₉⁻¹ is such a test. Drift itself is removed by exact arithmetic (Hoard 2026e, Proposition
 6.2), not by the cycle.


 4.5 The Hyperbolic Projection
 The source maps quaternion rotations into SL(2, ℂ), the double cover of the Lorentz group
 SO⁺(3, 1), so that branching unfolds in hyperbolic 3-space (BioChain AI 2026d, §3)
 [ESTABLISHED]. The precise statement is an inclusion: SU(2) ⊂ SL(2, ℂ) is the stabilizer of the
 base point of hyperbolic space, and there is no homomorphism SO(3) → SL(2, ℂ) splitting the
 cover (Fifteen Lemmas, Lemma 8, Proposition 6.3). The projection matrices are exact only if
 their entries lie in a ring such as the Gaussian integers ℤ[i]; with real entries drift returns. The
 release takes two exact routes instead: integer quaternions acting on a tree, and the rational
 ghost map (Hoard 2026e, Sections 7.1 and 7.6).


 4.6 Packet Layouts
 Three companion papers described the 64 bits of the packet, and they disagreed: a sandwich
 of an 8-bit North Halo, 16 scaling bits, a 32-bit quaternion core and an 8-bit South Halo
 (BioChain AI 2026e); four 16-bit phases X, Y, Z, W (BioChain AI 2026f); and 16 ﬁbres × 4
 phases (BioChain AI 2026g). The release adopts the Einstein Tile (Hoard 2026e, Deﬁnition 8.1),
 onto which the earlier layouts map: the sandwich's 32-bit core is the core word, its halos are
 the header and gate bytes, and its 16 scaling bits are the scale register. As ﬁeld maps they are
 [SUPERSEDED]; the 16-ﬁbre × 4-phase multiplex of BioChain AI (2026g) survives as an
 addressing of the same 64 bits.

 Problem 2 (Prime Norms Along a Lattice Line). [OPEN] Raised by the ﬁrst edition; Hoard
 (2026e), Section 8.3.

 Problem 6 (Flux Under Cell-Varying Drift). [OPEN] Raised by Fifteen Lemmas, companion

 Evidence. Edge by edge the statement fails, while the cross-sectional ﬂux was positive in every

 Problem 8 (The Alignment Handshake in Practice). [HEURISTIC] Raised by Hoard
 (2026e), Section 9.

 Does the alignment handshake let two independently built AI systems exchange states exactly

 Evidence. In simulation, signature matching succeeded in 499 of 500 trials and exact

 Test and decision rule (Protocol H). Two systems with independently trained encoders each map
 the same 1,000 preregistered objects into the lattice and run the handshake of Hoard (2026e),

 outcomes: the number of rounds, and the fraction of certiﬁed anchor pairs that a blinded
 human panel judges to denote the same object. Every certiﬁcate is exact by construction, so a
 single certiﬁcate that fails independent re-veriﬁcation refutes the implementation. The protocol
 is supported if at least 95 % of sessions certify and the panel agreement exceeds the
 agreement of randomly paired anchors by a margin ﬁxed in advance. Semantic agreement is
 reported separately, because no certiﬁcate can establish it.

 Dependence. None for the mathematics; the protocol tests the application.

 Problem 9 (A Prime Potential Without Primality). [OPEN] Raised by the ﬁrst edition.

 Is there a potential on the lattice, deﬁned without a primality test or a factorization of Q, whose
 minima are exactly the prime-quadrance nodes?

 Evidence. None. Under the Dual-Helix principle the sieve does not need one (Hoard 2026e,
 Section 3).

 Test and decision rule. The ﬁrst edition's falsiﬁable test: a proposed rule is refuted if its minima
 miss a prime quadrance or include a composite one, checked for all Q ≤ 10⁶.

 Dependence. None.

 Problem 10 (The Resolution Rule). [HEURISTIC] Raised by the source note, §6; Hoard
 (2026e), Section 8.2.

 Is δθ ≈ 1/√Q the right angular resolution of the lattice directions of norm at most Q?

 Evidence. The rule sets both precision horizons of the release, Q ≈ 10¹² for six decimal places
 and Q ≈ 2.3 × 10⁷ for cubit-anchored lattices. In three dimensions the lattice directions of
 norm at most Q are spaced about Q^{−3/4} apart in angle on average, while the largest gaps,
 next to directions of small norm, are of order Q^{−1/2}, so the rule matches the worst case
 rather than the average.

 Test and decision rule. Compute the covering radius of the primitive directions of norm at most
 Q on S² and S³ for Q up to 10⁶. The rule is conﬁrmed if √Q times the covering radius stays
 within a ﬁxed factor of 1 across that range, and replaced by the ﬁtted law otherwise; the
 horizons of Section 8.2 are then recomputed from it.

 Dependence. Only the horizons, which are tagged [HEURISTIC / COMPUTED]; no proof uses
 them.
