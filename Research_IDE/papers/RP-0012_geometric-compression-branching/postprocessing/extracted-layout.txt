          This paper investigates the geometric properties of a discrete 4 × 4 × 4 Euclidean lattice,

1    Introduction
The representation of 3D space using discrete voxel grids is fundamental to computational
physics and geometry processing. A standard 4 × 4 × 4 lattice contains 64 discrete nodes, where
coordinates (x, y, z) ∈ {0, 1, 2, 3}3 . These integer coordinates can be naturally extended to the
ring of Hurwitz integers (quaternions), providing an algebraic bridge between spatial geometry
and number theory.
   A critical challenge in such systems is identifying geometric redundancy—specifically, nodes
that lie strictly on the linear path between two other nodes. Traditional Euclidean methods rely
on distance calculations involving square roots, which introduce floating-point errors.
   We propose a rational approach using Quadrance (squared distance) and the Triple Quad For-
mula (TQF). We further explore how this static lattice supports a dynamic, scale-free branching
process defined by a directed 3-cycle mechanism (”The Rule of 3”) and Fibonacci scaling.

A Glossary of Terms
The following glossary bridges the gap between high-level academic theory and intuitive un-
derstanding, defining specialized terminology used in this research.

AfÏne Space A geometric space that has points and lines but no fixed origin point and no con-
    cept of ”angle” or ”distance” in the traditional sense. It focuses on the relationship between
    points (like ”middle” or ”parallel”). In this system, the Triple Quad Formula is derived in
    AfÏne Space to prove that the rules of linearity work regardless of lattice position.

Archimedes Function A specific algebraic formula used in Rational Trigonometry: (Q1 + Q2 +
     Q3 )2 − 2(Q21 + Q22 + Q23 ). If this function equates to zero, it proves that three points are lying
     perfectly on a straight line, allowing for data compression.

Asymptotic Stability A state in a dynamic system where, after a long period, the system settles
    into a specific, unchanging pattern. In this system, it refers to the ”Crystallization” phase
    where the Prime Sieve settles into a stable ”Prime Skeleton.”

Clifford-Strassen Toroid A complex, torus-shaped surface existing in 4-dimensional space. In
      this system, data streams are projected onto this 4D manifold, allowing waves to collide
      and form shapes naturally via curvature.
