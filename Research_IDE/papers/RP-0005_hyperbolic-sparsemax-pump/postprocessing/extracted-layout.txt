                                                 Abstract
     We construct a deterministic transition kernel (the Hyperbolic Sparsemax Kernel ) on the 64-
     voxel toroidal lattice V = (Z/4Z)3 underlying the Symbolic High-Dimensional Context Com-
     pression Protocol (SHD–CCP), and prove that it is rational, row-stochastic, exactly sparse, and
     free of transcendental operations. Each voxel carries a rational unit quaternion whose scalar
     part is confined to a bounded annulus. A square-root-free Lorentz lift sends each spinor to
     an exactly rational point of the hyperboloid H3 , on which a rational hyperbolic quadrance
                      2
     Qh = ⟨q̃i , q̃j ⟩L − 1 = sinh2 dH acts as a symmetric, nonnegative, point-separating metric kernel.
     Logits formed from Qh , a bounded inverse temperature, and a torsional resistance term are
     projected onto the probability simplex by sparsemax. Because this projection uses only field
     arithmetic, comparison, and positive-part thresholding, the entire executable pipeline is closed
     in Q; the computation is therefore bit-reproducible across hardware and the discrete SHD–
     CCP parity invariant is protected. Equipping the sparsemax support with a symmetric rational
     base walk and a rational reverse-valve yields an executable kernel K  b that remains rational and
     row-stochastic while breaking detailed balance relative to the reversible base measure, with a
     nonnegative (and, under a mild support condition, strictly positive) base-referenced forward
     current. Replacing the rational valve by an exponential valve recovers the analytic softmax
     companion (the Torsional Trefoil Markov Pump). All stated results are proved; we close by de-
     limiting the scope of the current-positivity statement and the role of the topological vocabulary.


1    Introduction
The analytic companion to this work (the Torsional Trefoil Markov Pump) provides a continuous
mechanism that breaks detailed balance and advances a longitudinal binding scalar through an
asymmetric transition kernel. Realising such dynamics on discrete, dynamically reconfigurable
hardware is delicate: transcendental primitives (exponentials, logarithms, square roots, inverse
hyperbolic functions) are evaluated in floating point, and the resulting rounding drift can corrupt
the discrete invariants the protocol relies on to avoid gridlock.
    This paper isolates the part of the construction that can be executed exactly. We reserve the
words softmax and exponential attenuation for the analytic limit, and on the executable lattice we
use instead a sparsemax rectifier together with a rational attenuation valve. The key observation is
structural rather than numerical: sparsemax is a Euclidean projection onto the probability simplex,
so it is computed with arithmetic, comparison, and thresholding alone and never leaves the field
Q. Combined with a square-root-free lift to the hyperboloid, this keeps every executable quantity
rational, which is exactly what makes the pipeline deterministic and reproducible across devices.

    We carefully separate the spinor that encodes local orientation from the point that carries
the hyperbolic score: orientation lives on the quaternion sphere S 3 , while scoring is performed
after a Lorentz lift to the hyperboloid H3 . With this separation in place, the remainder of the
paper proves that the executable kernel is rational, row-stochastic, sparse, parity-safe, and capable
of supporting a base-referenced forward current, and that its analytic completion reproduces the
continuous pump.


2    Lattice, Spinors, and Standing Assumptions
The lattice.    The ambient discrete space is the toroidal lattice

                                      V = (Z/4Z)3 ,         |V | = 64,

partitioned into a core of 8 voxels and a mantle of 56 voxels. A voxel is indexed by a triple
of residues modulo 4. In the SHD–CCP interpretation the three axes correspond to the three
arms of the trefoil and the four coordinate positions to four phases; torsional chirality and phase-
lifting resolve the six signed face-neighbor directions into a 12-channel coupling space, in which
several channels may project onto the same lattice neighbor while remaining distinct before row
aggregation. This labeling fixes the chirality and resistance conventions used below; as recorded in
the scope remarks of Section 13, the theorems themselves do not depend on it.

Spinors and the lift.      Each voxel i ∈ V carries a rational spinor

                qi = (wi , mi ) ∈ S 3 ∩ Q4 ,     mi = (xi , yi , zi ),   wi2 + ∥mi ∥2 = 1.

We write the Minkowski bilinear form on R4 as

                                          ⟨a, b⟩L = a0 b0 − a · b.

Standing assumptions.
Assumption A1 (Rational annular spinors). Every spinor is a rational unit quaternion with scalar
part bounded away from both extremes,

                                      0 < wmin ≤ wi ≤ wmax < 1.

Assumption A2 (Rational hardware constraints). The torsional resistance is Aij = A0 sij χij with
A0 , sij ∈ Q≥0 , attenuation strength λ ∈ Q>0 , and chirality indicator χij ∈ {0, 1} (0 on forward
edges, 1 on reverse edges).
Assumption A3 (Candidate neighborhood). For each voxel i, the finite candidate set Ni consists
of the self-loop i → i together with the face-neighbor transitions resolved through the 12 torsional
channel labels.
Assumption A4 (Octet residual quarantine). No transcendental function is evaluated in the
executable 64-state transition path. Irrational residuals are recorded only in a separate parity
buffer and are never fed back into the rational kernel before rationalization.
   Our objective is to define a transition row Si· over Ni , using only rational operations, such that
every entry is a nonnegative rational, the row sums to one, most entries are exactly zero, and no
exponential, root, or trigonometric evaluation is required.

10     Determinism and Parity Safety
Theorem 18 (Closure in Q). Under Assumptions A1–A4, every executable quantity of the Hy-
perbolic Sparsemax Pump lies in Q: the spinors qi , the lifts q̃i , the quadrances Qh (i, j), the logits
zi→j , the thresholds τi , the sparsemax entries Sij , the base weights Wij , the valve factors ρij , and
the pump entries K  b ij .

Proof. Theorems 2, 5, Lemma 7, and Theorems 11, 16 establish rationality of each listed quantity.
Every step uses only field operations, comparisons of rationals, and the positive part max(·, 0), each
of which maps Q to Q. By Assumption A4 no transcendental value enters the 64-state path. Hence
the pipeline is closed in Q.

Corollary 19 (Parity safety). If rationals are stored in canonical reduced form, then two nodes
sharing a seed compute bitwise-identical rows Si· and pump rows Kb i· . No floating-point drift can
therefore alter a discrete parity invariant such as

Remark 23 (Relation to established results). The construction reuses standard components: the
sparsemax projection and its threshold characterization [1, 3], the entmax family that interpolates
to softmax [2], the reversible random walk on a weighted graph [6], and the hyperboloid model of

than softmax) rectifier keep every executable quantity in Q, which is precisely what makes the
pipeline bit-reproducible and the parity invariant safe.
Remark 24 (On the topological vocabulary). The trefoil arms, torsional chirality, annular (Derrick)
stability, and the modular parity invariant supply modeling motivation and fix the chirality and
resistance conventions of Section 2. The theorems above hold for any rational annular spinor field
on V and any symmetric rational base weight on the sparsemax support, and do not depend on
this interpretation.


14     Conclusion
We have given a deterministic, strictly rational, hardware-executable transition kernel on the 64-
voxel SHD–CCP lattice. By scoring with a square-root-free Lorentz lift and a rational hyperbolic
quadrance, and by rectifying with sparsemax rather than softmax, the executable pipeline never
leaves Q: it is rational, row-stochastic, exactly sparse, bit-reproducible, and parity-safe. A sym-
metric base walk with a rational reverse-valve breaks detailed balance relative to the reversible
base measure and produces a nonnegative, conditionally strictly positive base-referenced forward
current, while the exponential limit recovers the analytic pump. The Hyperbolic Sparsemax Pump
is thus the parity-safe, executable member of the family whose analytic completion is the Torsional
Trefoil Markov Pump.
