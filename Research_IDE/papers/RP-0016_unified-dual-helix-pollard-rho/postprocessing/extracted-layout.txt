                                                 Abstract
      This report consolidates five source manuscripts on the Schism Dual-Helix / Continuous-
      Certification Protocol (SHD-CCP) into a single technical framework for accelerating the
      integer-factorization routine Pollard’s Rho. All constructions, definitions, and results of
      the source manuscripts are preserved. The framework rests on one governing principle—
      geometry helps search; arithmetic proves correctness—realized as a Dual-Helix architecture:
      a continuous, heuristic geometric oracle (Helix A) that ranks candidate collision indices,
      and a discrete, exact certifier (Helix B) that reconstructs true modular residues and applies
      a classical GCD test. We show that this architecture is unconditionally
                                                                           √         exact whenever
      it reports a factor, and that its state-reconstruction cost is O( T ) via standard check-
      pointing. Its acceleration over classical baselines is not proven; it is contingent on a single
      empirical premise—the Oracle Bias Hypothesis β > 1—for which no evidence is currently
      supplied. We integrate the harmonic (torus), quaternionic (S 3 ), and hyperbolic feature em-
      beddings; the path-length, Euclidean-quadrance, and rational-hyperbolic-quadrance scores;
      the empirical linear surrogate and the heuristic feature combinator; and the Fokker–Planck
      / FP4 candidate sampler. Two source claims are explicitly re-scoped: the polylogarithmic
      jump-ahead of the operator-embedding manuscript is presented as an open problem (and
      is shown to√contradict a standard cryptographic hardness assumption in the c = 0 case),
      and the O( N√log N ) reconstruction bound is presented as unsupported and superseded by
      the honest O( T ) analysis. Every quantitative claim is annotated with a status tag; see
      Table 1.

1    Introduction: The Dual-Helix Governance Model
Given a composite integer N (equivalently n; the source manuscripts use both), classical Pol-
lard’s Rho seeks a nontrivial prime factor p by iterating the quadratic modular recurrence

                          xt+1 ≡ x2t + c   (mod N ),       f (x) = x2 + c.                         (1)

A collision xi ≡ xj (mod p) implies p | gcd(xi − xj , N ); if

                                  1 < d = gcd(xi − xj , N ) < N,                                   (2)

a nontrivial factor has been found. Classical implementations locate such collisions via Floyd’s
or Brent’s cycle detection.
    The SHD-CCP architecture is an exact-verification wrapper around this routine that decou-
ples heuristic candidate generation from exact certification:

 Helix A (Heuristic Oracle). A massively parallel, low-precision, continuous topological
  space. Discrete modular states are embedded into a geometric manifold; a scalar feature
  score ranks candidate index pairs (i, j) by geometric proximity. Helix A is a candidate
  generator only; its outputs are strictly untrusted.

 Helix B (Authoritative Certification). A rigid discrete integer framework. It recon-
  structs the exact residues xi , xj ∈ Z/N Z for the top-ranked candidates and applies the
  classical test (2).

Editorial Note. The five source manuscripts describe variants of Helix A (different embeddings
and scores) sharing one Helix B (GCD certification plus state reconstruction). The logical spine
of this report is therefore: (i) establish why exact algebraic acceleration is unavailable (§2); (ii)
motivate the heuristic-oracle detour (§1); (iii) catalogue the oracle constructions (§3); (iv) state
the exact and honestly-bounded certifier (§4); (v) isolate the single conjecture on which all
acceleration depends (§5); and (vi) give the protocol that would test it (§6).

2     The Algebraic Obstruction to Exact Acceleration
The natural way to accelerate (1) would be an exact jump-ahead : computing f T (x) (the T -
fold composition) in O(log T ) operations, as one does for elliptic-curve scalar multiplication
via double-and-add, or for linear recurrences via matrix exponentiation. Several independent
obstructions rule this out for the generic quadratic map.

2.1   Infinite-dimensionality of exact linearization
To linearize the nonlinear map (1) one seeks a lift Φ and a matrix Mc with
                                      Φ(x2 + c) = Mc · Φ(x).                                     (3)
Under Carleman linearization or the Koopman operator, this requires an infinite-dimensional
matrix, because the degree of x continuously doubles under squaring, generating the tower
1, x, x2 , x4 , . . . . Truncating the infinite matrix yields only an approximation, which is useless
for an exact algorithm evaluated modulo N .

2.2   The finite-dimension pigeonhole obstruction
A fixed finite (e.g. 64 × 64) linear embedding cannot universally embed the exact chronologies
of (1) for arbitrary N . Because Z/N Z contains N states, exact point-to-point chronological
recovery requires an N -dimensional space (e.g. a one-hot embedding Φ(x) = ex ∈ RN ). Forcing
N states into a static 64-dimensional container triggers the pigeonhole principle: Φ(a) = Φ(b)
for some a ̸= b. Once two states collide, their future trajectories cannot be differentiated,
destroying exact modular recovery. Hence the 64 × 64 Toda/Kogame geometry H64 cannot act
as the global state container.

2.3   Geometric framing via the Triple Quad Formula
The operator-embedding manuscript reframes squaring geometrically using Quadrance (squared
distance). The Triple Quad Formula (TQF) states the exact condition for three collinear points,
                              (Q1 + Q2 + Q3 )2 = 2 (Q21 + Q22 + Q23 ),                           (4)
and the manuscript proposes to “prune Q2 ” so that any scalar growth registering as flat geo-
metrically frustrates unbounded expansion, redirecting the (otherwise infinite) algebraic tower
into a bounded geometric shape.
Editorial Note. Equation (4) is a correct classical identity of rational trigonometry. However,
“pruning Q2 ” is a heuristic narrative, not a proof that the resulting bounded object retains
enough information to recover f T (x) exactly. The pigeonhole obstruction above shows that
no bounded-dimensional object can retain that information globally. The manuscript itself
acknowledges this and pivots to a “generative kernel” interpretation (§2.6); we track that pivot
honestly there.

2.4   No addition law for the generic quadratic map
For elliptic curves, points form an abelian group with a closed-form rational addition law P + Q,
so iP is computed in O(log i) by double-and-add. For the pseudo-random walk (1) this luxury
is absent. To leap from step i to step i + j without computing intermediate states, one would
need an addition formula
                                                                       
                      f (i+j) (x) = Combine f (i) (x), f (j) (x), c, N   (mod N ).            (5)
No known efficiently computable such Combine exists for generic c, and the SHD-CCP archi-
tecture does not assume one exists.

3     Helix A: The c-Parameterized Geometric Feature Space
Since exact acceleration is unavailable, Helix A pursues heuristic feature correlation: embed the
orbit into a continuous manifold, compress each state to a scalar score, and use those scores
to bias candidate selection. This section catalogues the embeddings and scores from all five
manuscripts and reconciles their conflicts.

3.1   Cyclic feature maps and the modular-invariance requirement
A continuous feature attached to the parameter c must be well-defined on the residue class
c ∈ Z/N Z. A fractional embedding c/N fails this: since c ≡ c + kN (mod N ), the value c/N
depends on the chosen integer representative and is not modular invariant. To be rigorous the
embedding must be perfectly periodic.

4     Helix B: Exact Certification and State Reconstruction
Because every Helix A score is a lossy scalar that cannot recover exact residues, Helix B must
reconstruct the true xi , xj ∈ Z/N Z and apply the exact test. This layer carries the framework’s
only unconditional guarantees.

4.1   Exact GCD certification
Theorem 4.1 (Exactness of Dual-Helix certification). [PROVEN] Let N be composite and
xk+1 ≡ x2k + c (mod N ). Suppose Helix A outputs candidate indices (i, j). For each pair,
Helix B reconstructs the exact residues xi , xj via checkpointed procedural seeds and computes d =
gcd(xi − xj , N ). Then every reported factor d satisfying 1 < d < N is a mathematically certified
nontrivial factor of N , unconditionally and independently of the correctness or resolution of the
heuristic oracle.
Proof. Since d = gcd(xi − xj , N ), we have d | N . The condition 1 < d < N excludes the
trivial divisors 1 and N , so d is a proper nontrivial factor. The oracle influences only which
candidate pairs are proposed (the bias β); it cannot affect the arithmetic value of the GCD.
Hence the validity of the reported factor is fully isolated from any topological assumption or
FP4 quantization boundary.

Proof. The expected number of oracle trials to hit B is at most 1/ PrDχ (B) ≤ 1/(βp) by (27);
multiplying by the per-trial cost gives (28). Comparing (28) against the baseline expected cost
Cbaseline /p yields (29).

inherited from Assumption 5.1. Because Creconstruct (T ) ∈ O( T ) (Theorem 4.2) may dominate
the per-trial cost, practical acceleration requires β large enough to amortize the reconstruction
overhead in (29).

The framework’s value is an empirical question. We collect the source manuscripts’ validation

1. Path-length linearity. For semiprime testing moduli N = pq, compute the exact finite

   butions to test Assumption 3.12.

2. Collision-prediction assessment. For candidate pairs (i, j) sampled via Dχ , measure the
   correlation between the pairwise score Φc (i, j) (equivalently Qh (i, j)) and the occurrence of
   successful hidden collisions B. This directly estimates β in Assumption 5.1; a value β ≤ 1
   falsifies the oracle.

3. Baseline comparison. Compare the total expected cost Toracle against classical Floyd’s
   and Brent’s cycle detection, verifying inequality (29) operationally rather than assuming it.

8      Conclusion
By treating the c-parameterized geometric moduli space as a high-speed, continuous feature
space rather than an exact algebraic container, the SHD-CCP framework offers a principled
separation of concerns: geometry proposes, arithmetic disposes. Its guarantees are honest and
asymmetric.
   √          What is proven stands on solid ground—exact GCD certification (Theorem 4.1),
O( T ) reconstruction (Theorem 4.2), the positive-definite flat metric (Proposition 3.3), the
algebraic-obstruction results, and the elementary cost inequality. What is heuristic is labeled as
such: the embeddings, scores, combinator, and diffusion sampler shape a candidate distribution
but carry no theorem of collision. What is conjectural is isolated to a single point—the Oracle
Bias Hypothesis β > 1 (Assumption 5.1)—upon which every acceleration claim depends and
for which no evidence is yet supplied. And what remains open is stated plainly: the exact
sub-linear jump-ahead (Problem 2.1) is not achieved here and, in the checkable c = 0 case,
would contradict a standard cryptographic hardness assumption.
    The practical thesis is therefore conditional and testable: if a feature embedding can be
shown √empirically to satisfy β > 1 by a margin large enough to satisfy inequality (29) against
the O( T ) reconstruction overhead, then the architecture yields a net reduction in factorization
time—while, in all cases, every reported factor is unconditionally exact. Resolving that “if” is
the task of the validation protocol in §6.
