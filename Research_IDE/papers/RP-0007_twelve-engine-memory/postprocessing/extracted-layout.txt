  Summary
  The proposal works as a memory architecture. Below are the three results and the caveat
  that limits each one.

       Implied fourth = parity engine (M1). Each group of three data engines plus its implied
       fourth satisfies H₁H₂H₃H₄ = 1, so any one of the four can be rebuilt exactly from the
       other three. The hierarchy survives the loss of one engine per group (three in all), but it
       costs 25% of capacity: only 9 of the 12 engines carry new data. Two losses in the same
       group cannot be recovered.
       Offload onto the inverse tier system is exact (M2). The tier transition word U, U⁻¹, U,
       U⁻¹ is its own reversed inverse, so the exact inverse tier system is the same tier system
       run backwards. The net rotation is the identity, the spinor product is exactly 1, and the
       radial scale goes 31/3 → 3/31. Rotating Hurwitz-integer data through a tier keeps its
       norm and is undone exactly, so nothing grows in bit width. Caveat: offloading an
       implied fourth gives back only the product of its three parents, not their separate
       histories.
       The free 120° phase (M3). If the three phase angles are three separate time slots, the
       third slot is a genuinely free channel: +50% over two. If they are three phasors on one
       complex carrier, it is not free (1 + ω + ω² = 0). Under the 120° rotation, three slots split
       into one invariant line plus one rotating plane. The invariant line is exactly the position
       of the implied fourth (the coset the rotation fixes). Recommended use: carry the
       implied-fourth (parity) traffic, or the reference clock, in the third phase so both data
       phases stay 100% data.
       Counts. 12 × 6 = 72 tier slots and 12 × 60 = 720 outer frames match the 72 middle-gear
       and 720 outer frames. These are counting correspondences, not theorems.
       Limits that remain. Pigeonhole bounds still apply: the hierarchy adds erasure tolerance
       and phase throughput, not stored information beyond the bits actually written.

  Every claim below is checked in verify_memory.py (log in §6).

  1. Reading of the proposal
  The analysis rests on these assumptions. Each one is the most direct reading of the
  request in terms of the fifteen lemmas.

       A1 — six tiers. "6-tier" means the core plus the five toroidal tiers of Lemma 1 (Rₙ =
       5n²+15n+15, Wₙ = 16−2n, WΣ = 60). The "5-manifold" label does not enter any statement
       below. If it is meant to add structure, that is listed as open item O3.
       A2 — engine state. An engine's memory state is its frame holonomy H, a unit
       quaternion (Lemmas 7 and 12), together with its 64-bit SHD-CCP packet.
       A3 — implied fourth. The implied fourth of three engines is the closure H₄ = (H₁H₂H₃)⁻¹
       of Lemma 7. Its geometric counterpart is the Klein-four coset closure of the FCC
       interleave, in which any three cosets determine the fourth.
       A4 — phases. The memory cycle has three 120° phase angles. Two are in use: onload
       and offload at the orthogonal handshake steps.

  Each implied fourth is itself a full six-tier engine, as the request requires. It receives data

  2. Proposition M1 — the implied fourth is a parity engine
  Statement. Let H₁, H₂, H₃ be the holonomies of three data engines and set H₄ = (H₁H₂H₃)⁻¹.
  Then H₁H₂H₃H₄ = 1, and each Hᵢ is determined by the other three:

       H₁ = (H₂H₃H₄)⁻¹
       H₂ = H₁⁻¹ H₄⁻¹ H₃⁻¹
       H₃ = H₂⁻¹ H₁⁻¹ H₄⁻¹
       H₄ = (H₁H₂H₃)⁻¹

  Proof. The product H₁H₂H₃H₄ = 1 is a relation in a group, so it is preserved by cyclic shifts:
  conjugating by H₁ gives H₂H₃H₄H₁ = 1, and so on for each rotation. Solving each cyclic form
  for its last factor gives the four formulas. ∎

  Consequences.

   1. Single-erasure tolerance per group. One lost engine in each of groups A, B and C is
      fully recoverable: up to three losses across the hierarchy.
   2. Two erasures in one group are fatal. If H₁ and H₂ are both lost, every pair (X, X⁻¹T) with
       T = (H₃H₄)⁻¹ is consistent with the record. The check found 50 distinct consistent pairs
       out of 50 trials.
   3. Capacity. The fourth carries no new information, so only 9 of the 12 engines carry data
      (75%).
   4. Order matters. Quaternion multiplication does not commute, so every engine in the
      hierarchy must use the same multiplication order. This is the open order convention of
      Lemma 12. The same convention must be fixed system-wide.
   5. Additive form. The geometric version is the FCC coset closure: (1,1,0) + (1,0,1) + (0,1,1) =
      (2,2,2) ≡ (0,0,0) mod 2. This is parity in the plain XOR sense, and it is the same erasure
      code as the holonomy version.

  Optional 13th engine. A global fourth-of-fourths G = (F_A F_B F_C)⁻¹ adds exactly one
  new recovery case: losing a data engine and its own fourth (for example A1 and F_A). G
  and F_B, F_C rebuild F_A, and then F_A rebuilds A1. It does not rescue two lost data
  engines in one group, because the group's product gives one equation in two unknowns.

  3. Proposition M2 — offload onto the inverse tier system is exact
  Statement. Onload carries data from the core out through tiers 0 → 4 by the transition
  word W = U₃U₂U₁U₀, where U_even = U and U_odd = U⁻¹ (Lemma 1/7). The exact inverse
  tier system is W⁻¹ = U₀⁻¹U₁⁻¹U₂⁻¹U₃⁻¹. Then:

   1. The inverse system is the same system. Reversing the alternating word U, U⁻¹, U, U⁻¹
      and inverting each letter gives U, U⁻¹, U, U⁻¹ again. So the inverse tier system has the
       same transitions as the original, traversed tier 4 → 0. No separate inverse hardware is
       needed.
   2. The net rotation is the identity. W = I, and the spinor word is h·h̄·h·h̄ = 1 exactly, with h
       = (1−i+j+k)/2. Onload followed by inverse offload therefore leaves no residual rotation:
       no sign flip and no 2π ambiguity.
   3. The radial scale is exactly reversed. R₀ = 15 and R₄ = 155, so onload scales by R₄/R₀ =
      31/3 and inverse offload by 3/31. Both are exact rationals.
   4. Data is preserved at fixed width. Left multiplication q ↦ hq is a norm-preserving
       bijection of the Hurwitz integers, and h̄(hq) = q. A data quaternion passed through any
       number of tier rotations therefore stays a Hurwitz integer of the same norm and the
       same bit width. This is the contrast with Lemma 13: products of data with data grow in
       bit width, but rotation by unit spinors does not.

  Proof. Items 1–3 are direct computation (checked exactly). For item 4, h is a Hurwitz unit
  (N(h) = 1). Multiplication by a unit maps the Hurwitz order onto itself, and N(hq) =
  N(h)N(q) = N(q). The inverse map is multiplication by h̄ = h⁻¹. ∎

  What the implied fourth gives back. Offloading F_A returns exactly F_A = (A1A2A3)⁻¹:
  the product, not the three parents. The history-loss count from the system analysis
  applies: 24³ = 13,824 chains of Hurwitz units collapse to 24 products. To read an individual
  parent, three of the four engines in its group are needed (M1).

  Verified. Transition-word identities and W = I with integer matrices; spinor word exactly 1;
  5000 random Hurwitz data quaternions survive h, then h̄, exactly.

  4. Twelve cubes, 72 angles, 720 frames
  Geometry of 12 = 3 × 4 (THEOREM). The FCC lattice built by the 3:1 interleave has 12
  nearest neighbours, the cuboctahedron vectors (±1, ±1, 0) and their permutations. They
  split by which coordinate is zero into three planar groups of four. In each group the four
  vectors sum to zero, so any three determine the fourth: the additive form of "three
  engines plus their implied fourth". The 120° rotation about (1,1,1) carries each group onto
  another, which is the same symmetry that cycles the three phase angles (§5). The twelve-
  engine hierarchy is therefore the cuboctahedral shell of one engine in the tessellation,
  with each group of four lying in one coordinate plane.

  Counts (CORRESPONDENCE).


     Count                              Value                          Matches

     12 engines × 6 tiers               72                             72 middle-gear frames (720/10)

     12 engines × WΣ = 60               720                            720 outer frames

     72 slots in 10 inner blocks        8, 7, 7, 7, 7, 8, 7, 7, 7, 7   the Aₗ block pattern

     360° / 72                          5° per slot                    —


  These numbers agree, but no lemma forces them, so they are tagged as correspondences.

  Which "72" is meant. Two different 72s appear in the corpus:

       the count 72 = 12 × 6 tier slots (above);
       the angle 72°, the dihedral angle of the {4,3,5} hyperbolic cubic honeycomb (five cubes
       round each edge).

  The twelve-engine hierarchy gives the first. The second is the stacking angle that makes
  the cube tessellation hyperbolic and unbounded. They agree only through 360°/5 = 72°
  versus 360°/72 = 5°, which is a coincidence of numerals rather than an identity. The
  monograph should say which is intended wherever "72 geometric angles" appears.


  5. Proposition M3 — the unused 120° phase
  Whether the third phase is "free" depends on what a phase angle physically is. There are
  two readings, and they give opposite answers.

Memory in the Twelve-Engine Hierarchy




  (a) Phases as time or frame slots (free). If the three 120° phases are three disjoint slots in
  the cycle, each can carry a full packet independently. Using the third raises throughput by
  3/2 at no cost to the other two.

  (b) Phases as phasors on one complex carrier (not free). If data is written as amplitudes
  on e⁰, e^{2πi/3} and e^{4πi/3} of one carrier, then 1 + ω + ω² = 0. The carrier has only two
  real dimensions, so three phasors hold just two independent numbers, and the third
  phase is already spent.

  Decomposition (THEOREM). Take three real channels (a, b, c), one per phase. Let s = a + b
  + c and z = a + ωb + ω²c. Then:

       (a, b, c) is recovered exactly from s and z;
       a 120° cyclic shift of the channels leaves s fixed and multiplies z by ω.

  So three phases = one invariant line ⊕ one rotating plane. Checked in 1000 random cases.

  Match with the implied fourth. The FCC interleave has the same structure. Under the
  120° rotation about (1,1,1), the corner coset o₀ is fixed and the three face cosets are cycled.
  The rotating plane carries the three data engines, which trade places under rotation. The
  invariant line carries what every rotation fixes, and that is the position of the implied
  fourth.

  Recommended use of the third phase.

   1. Parity phase (preferred). Onload and offload the implied fourth's traffic in the third
       phase. Both data phases then carry 100% data. Without this, parity takes a quarter of
       the two working phases, so data throughput is ×4/3 relative to sharing.
   2. Clock phase. Alternatively, carry the reference clock (the caesium 30-pulse window of
       Lemma 13/Hoard 2026c) in the third phase, so timing never competes with data.
   3. Data phase. Under reading (a) only, use it as a third data channel: ×3/2 throughput, but
       parity then has to share again.

  Constraint from Lemma 8. The eight-step cycle has apex steps k = 2 and 6, which are 180°
  apart, not 120°. The 120° structure comes from the order-3 tier rotation, not from the
  eight-step cycle. The two align over a joint super-cycle of lcm(8, 3) = 24 steps. The phase
  plan must be written on that 24-step cycle: open item O2.

  Limits that do not move.

       Pigeonhole. No arrangement stores more than the bits written. The hierarchy buys
       erasure tolerance and phase throughput, not extra capacity.
       History loss. A fourth stores a product; histories are not recoverable from it alone.
       Two erasures in one group are unrecoverable without an extra code.
       Order convention. Quaternion multiplication does not commute, so one multiplication
       order must be fixed for the whole hierarchy (Lemma 12).

  Open items.

       O1 — Physical meaning of a phase angle. Slot (reading a) or phasor (reading b)? This
       decides whether the third phase is free.
       O2 — 24-step super-cycle. Write the onload, offload and third-phase schedule on
       lcm(8, 3) = 24 steps, so the 120° phases meet the k = 2, 6 apex steps of Lemma 8.
       O3 — "5-manifold". Does it add structure beyond the six tiers? Nothing here depends
       on it.
       O4 — 13th engine. Include the global fourth-of-fourths or not? The cost is one engine
       for one extra recovery case.
       O5 — Which "72". The count 72 or the honeycomb angle 72°?
