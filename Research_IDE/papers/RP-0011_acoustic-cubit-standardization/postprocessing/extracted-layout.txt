       Abstract
       This report formalizes the local acoustic cubit as a continued installment of the SHD-CCP research series
       and an addition to The Geometric Engine of Time. Defined as one-sixteenth of an acoustic wavelength at a
       fixed calibration frequency, C₀ = v/(16 f_cal), the cubit is realized at 20 °C with an expanded uncertainty of
       0.22 mm (k = 2) from the Cramer (1993) real-air equation, and it grows by 0.98 mm per kelvin. Every
       number and identity of the October 2026 technical report was recomputed in code: all seven of its
       mathematical results hold once stated precisely, five after correction, while its pressure coefficient is at
       least 55 times too large. The stellar, galactic and holonomy extensions lie at least five orders of magnitude
       below the realization uncertainty and cannot be tested on Earth, and the 53.22 cm cosmological cubit
       carries no evidential weight under a look-elsewhere test. Against climate data, calibration at the annual
       mean temperature fits historical cubits no better than chance; a cold-season variant at two-thirds of a 432
       Hz wavelength fits the Egyptian and Nippur cubits and is preregistered as hypothesis H1b. The Geometric
       Engine of Time supplies the most accurate method at four points: exact caesium-cycle bookkeeping, the
       Triple Quad Formula (TQF) identity in rational arithmetic, a 30-node wheel that tests 20 % fewer prime
       candidates than the base-6 wheel, and a 0.5 s closure window that holds whole periods of the caesium
       clock and of both series carriers. Three preregistered protocols state the measurements that would confirm
       or refute each open claim.

 1 Introduction
 The cubit is among the oldest attested standards of length, yet its value differed by about a quarter between cultures.
 This proposal asks whether one acoustic rule can explain part of that spread, and builds a usable acoustic unit whatever
 the answer.


 1.1 Metrological Background
 Since 2019 the SI fixes the speed of light at 299,792,458 m/s and the caesium hyperfine frequency at 9,192,631,770 Hz.
 The metre is therefore independent of the medium in which it is used.

 An acoustic wavelength is not. In an ideal gas, λ = v/f with v proportional to √T, so a length tied to λ grows by about
 0.17 % per kelvin near 20 °C. For an acoustic array this is a feature: element spacings expressed in wavelengths keep
 the array's phase geometry fixed when the air changes, while spacings in metres do not.

 The technical report built on this idea as part of the SHD-CCP series, drawing its frequencies from The Geometric Engine
 of Time (Hoard 2026a). It defined the cubit as λ/16 at 40 Hz, noted that this reproduces values in the historical range,
 and added non-local factors intended to make the unit valid across astrophysical environments. This proposal keeps the
 first idea, tests the second, and quantifies the third.

 3.3 Voxel Partition and Phase
          Proposition 3.1 (Voxel Partition and Phase). [PROVEN] Report Proposition 2.2.
          Let a cube of edge L = 4C₀ be divided into 4 × 4 × 4 voxels of edge C₀. Then:

            1. one voxel occupies 1/64 of the volume, and its face 1/16 of a cross-section;
            2. a displacement of one cubit along a plane wave advances the phase by π/8;
            3. the cube edge equals λ/4, the distance from a pressure node to the adjacent antinode of a standing wave.

 7.1 Why Matching Rods to Temperatures Proves Nothing on Its Own

 7.2 Method
 Status: [EMPIRICAL / COMPUTED]. New in this proposal.

   1. For each sourced cubit L and rule divisor d (640, 648 or 768), the required temperature T* solves v_id(T*) = dL for
      dry air.
   2. T* is compared with two climate anchors at the provenance: the annual mean and the coldest-month (January)
      mean, from modern station normals (Section 2.3).
   3. A cubit counts as a hit when the rule closest to the anchor gives |T* − T_anchor| ≤ 2 °C. The tolerance reflects
      Holocene variability: global mean temperature 6500 years ago was only 0.7 °C (0.3 to 1.8 °C) above the 19th
      century (Kaufman et al. 2020).
   4. The chance expectation is the probability that a length drawn uniformly from 44 to 57 cm scores a hit under the
      same rules and anchors. Tail probabilities use the Poisson-binomial distribution over the eight independent cubits;
      the King's Chamber value duplicates the Great Pyramid cubit and is shown but not counted.
   5. Humidity is ignored in the baseline. At 50 % relative humidity, required temperatures fall by about 0.65 °C (13.25
      °C dry versus 12.60 °C humid for the Great Pyramid cubit under C₂/₃), which changes no conclusion.
