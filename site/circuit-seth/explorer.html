Understanding $d$-Depth Circuit-SETH & Why $d = 2$ Is the Super-Linear CNF Cliff

Formal Conjecture ($d$-depth circuit-SETH): There is no algorithm that solves $n$-variable, $n^{1+\delta}$-gate, $d$-depth, unbounded fan-in circuit-SAT in time $(2-\varepsilon)^n$ for any $\delta, \varepsilon > 0$. Currently this is open even for $d = 2$.

1. Why $d = 2$ Is Unbounded CNF-SAT

A depth-$2$ unbounded fan-in circuit has Layer 1 consisting of $m$ unbounded-width $\text{OR}$ gates (clauses) and Layer 2 consisting of a single top-level $\text{AND}$ gate: $$\Phi(x) = \bigwedge_{i=1}^{n^{1+\delta}} \left(\bigvee_{j \in C_i} \ell_{i,j}\right)$$ Unlike $k$-SAT (where fan-in is bounded by $k$), each clause can touch up to $n$ variables at once.

2. The $\delta = 0$ vs. $\delta > 0$ Phase Cliff

When the number of clauses is linear ($m = c n$, i.e. $\delta = 0$), Schuler (2005) and Calabro–Impagliazzo–Paturi (2006) proved CNF-SAT is solvable in $2^{(1 - 1/\mathcal{O}(\log c))n} = (2-\varepsilon_c)^n$ time. The moment clauses scale super-linearly as $m = n^{1+\delta}$ ($\delta > 0$), $m/n = n^\delta \to \infty$, so $\varepsilon_c \to 0$ and the Sparsification Lemma fails!

3. Connection to Theorem 5.11 ($P = NP$)

In Hoard (2026i), Theorem 5.11 proves $P = NP$ if and only if SAT admits a polynomial-time Integrable Encoding $(\text{enc}, \text{eval})$ (Def. 5.10). Falsifying $d=2$ circuit-SETH requires compressing $n^{1+\delta}$ global clause intersections into a sub-$2^n$ procedural evaluation.
